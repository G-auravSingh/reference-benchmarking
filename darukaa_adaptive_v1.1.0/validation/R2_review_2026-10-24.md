## Decision needed: what pressure is compared against

The three options below assume one rule throughout: the absolute value is always reported beside the score, whichever option is chosen.

| Option | Reference for P4 | Cropland control | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (current, D-23-9) | All land in the same ecoregion ring | 100 | Answers "is this site worse than its neighbours?". Matches a 3Bee-style control comparison [VENDOR]. | In intensively used regions, high pressure scores well. Not comparable across regions. |
| B | Least-disturbed blocks in the same ecoregion (the same gHM ladder as the condition pool). Worst = contract maximum or regional p95, whichever is higher. | low | Same reference concept as condition (SEEA-EA: departure from a low-pressure state; TOR p.150) [STD]. Comparable across regions. | In fully converted ecoregions the "least disturbed 10%" is itself disturbed, so scores are generous there. The ladder stage is recorded. |

**Recommendation: B for the P4 score, with C classes shown beside it where published, and A kept as a contextual "versus surroundings" figure.** This changes D-23-9, so it needs your approval before implementation.

## Proposals for decision

**P-26-1. Floor and search widening for the low-pressure reference.** All three steps run within the same RESOLVE ecoregion.
1. A block qualifies only if it is at least 50% natural in 2020. This applies at both stages, strict (≥ 90%) and quantile.
2. If fewer than 20 qualifying blocks are found, the ring is widened from 25 to 50 to 100 km. Every ring still excludes all sites plus 1 km.
3. If there are still too few, P4 is not scored (`reference_insufficient`). The report then shows only the absolute classes and the "versus surroundings" figure.

The radius used is recorded. Effect: Ludhiana is either compared with genuinely natural land in its ecoregion (likely the Shivalik foothills, 50–100 km away; *to be verified by run*) or left unscored. Neither outcome is a flattering 100.

**P-26-2. Fire-year validity.**
- A pixel-year counts as observed if any month records a burn, or if at least 6 months have valid data.
- Today the rule requires all 12 months, which removes most monsoon-region years.
- Unobserved pixel-years stay masked; they are never counted as unburned.
