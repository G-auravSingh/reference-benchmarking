3. Design principles

  • Segmentation/clustering. An EMU is decided on ecological grounds (Ecological stratification based on GEOBIA - Image segmentation) and then tuned as per logistics.
  • Spatial contiguity is a structural guarantee for segmentation-derived EMUs — verified by a hard runtime check, not assumed.
  • Device coverage is a hard guarantee. Alls EMU this pipeline delineates are representatively covered.
  • Distribution shown alongside the worst-case flag, never the flag alone — a single declining tile inside an otherwise-healthy EMU should never read as "no improvement happening" project-wide.

4. Site selection methodology

Method: Google Earth Engine (GEE) Simple Non-Iterative Clustering (SNIC) image segmentation algorithm over the tessellated grid, reconciled with already-declared ecological anchors.

Minimum-mapping-unit auto-tune: 44 raw SNIC segments (after connected-component splitting: 70 genuinely contiguous pieces) → auto-tuned threshold 22 → 4 final segments (converged to target).

7b. Soil chemistry sampling design

A real, one-time composite sampling design, collected once during the final deployment week — not part of the weekly audiomoth/camera trap rotation above

  • Design: 4-5 corner points + 1 centre point per EMU, composited into real soil chemistry samples (NPK, pH, EC and similar parameters).
  • Corner placement: Verified to be a real, already-tessellated candidate belonging to its stated EMU. The centre is each EMU's own specified candidate, or its real geometric centroid
  • Coverage: 31 real points across 6 EMUs (Fruit Forest, Wetland, SEG01, SEG02, SEG03, SEG04).
